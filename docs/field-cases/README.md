# BRONCO Field Cases

BRONCO Field Cases are public, evidence-linked stress tests of the BRONCO measurement model against real evaluation, reproducibility and auditability problems.

They are **not verdicts, accusations or vendor rankings**. A field case exists to make the measurement question explicit, separate observations from interpretation, identify what would falsify the concern, and update the record when better evidence appears.

## Required case structure

Each field case should record at least:

- **Status** — open, partially resolved, resolved, superseded or archived.
- **Scope** — the specific measurement claim or evaluation being examined.
- **Research question** — what the case is trying to determine.
- **Measurement object** — what system/result is actually under discussion.
- **Known provenance** — documented experimental conditions and source-backed facts.
- **Missing or partial provenance** — information needed to interpret or reproduce the result.
- **Potential confounders** — factors that may affect the result without being the claimed measurand.
- **Evidence classification** — measured/documented, correlated, inference, or open question.
- **Good-faith falsifier** — evidence that would materially weaken or resolve the concern.
- **Public source record** — primary sources and public discussion.
- **Corrections and updates** — changes made when evidence improves.
- **Current assessment** — the narrowest conclusion justified by the evidence.

A case should never convert an unresolved measurement question into an allegation of intent.

## Relationship to the BRONCO measurement model

The field-case framework applies the same principle used by the core [BRONCO Measurement Model](../measurement-model.md): a result is not just a scalar score. It is a measurement result interpreted together with system-identity evidence, experimental conditions, uncertainty/variability, failure accounting, provenance and validity limits.

Field Cases therefore serve two purposes:

1. test whether the abstract BRONCO model survives contact with real public evaluation systems; and
2. feed discovered failure modes, missing fields and falsifiers back into the research specification before BRONCO becomes a benchmark product.
